## Direction

The setting should feel like realistic industrial sci-fi rather than a mystery
box. The central conflict is not a magical mineral or a strange machine. It is
a political fight over strategic materials, infrastructure authority, civilian
survival, liability, and who gets to define legality on a remote frontier.

The old term Loom remains useful only as field slang. The formal system is the
Basin Stabilization Grid, an old human-built industrial-control network in
Aster Basin. Kestrel technicians call it the Loom because its buried power,
pump, refinery, road-lock, and depot routes cross the basin like a woven mesh.
It is not alien, sentient, prophetic, or limitless. It is damaged
infrastructure with bad authorization records and dangerous emergency logic.

## Core Premise

Aster Basin is a remote settled region on the frontier world Caldera. It was
opened during a resource boom, partially abandoned after a lethal industrial
disaster, then quietly kept alive by Meridian communities that learned how to
maintain shallow power taps, pumps, and depots outside official channels.

Decades later, Asterite becomes strategically important again. Orison Resource
Combine moves to reopen extraction. The Sable Accord sends forces to prevent a
corporate monopoly from becoming a military imbalance. Treaty Oversight tries
to protect the legal order and its own buried mistakes. Kestrel arrives as a
civilian survey expedition and discovers that every faction has been given only
part of the truth.

The campaign question is: who controls life-supporting infrastructure when that
same infrastructure can also become strategic leverage?

## Interstellar Travel And Messaging

Human space is connected by the Transit Thread: a network of high-energy beam
arrays, fusion pushers, magsail braking fields, propellant depots, orbital
shipyards, and courier relays. Ships do not jump. They ride planned transfer
windows between known systems, accelerate along a certified beam corridor,
coast for months or years, then brake against destination infrastructure.

The Transit Thread is fast enough to hold an interstellar civilization together
and slow enough to make frontier politics dangerous. A passenger cutter or
priority courier can cross a short route in months. Heavy freight, military
equipment, habitat modules, and refinery parts often take years from contract
to arrival because mass, braking capacity, launch windows, and insurance all
matter.

Messages move through the Spindle Net, an FTL signaling layer built into major
Transit Thread nodes. Spindle stations exchange tiny authenticated state changes
through paired metric resonators anchored in massive, shielded facilities. The
effect can carry bits, not mass, energy, people, or sensor pictures. It is not a
radio anyone can improvise in a field truck. It is scarce public infrastructure
with bandwidth measured in priority packets, legal seals, market notices,
military orders, emergency votes, and compressed evidence hashes.

Spindle messaging is the setting's one deliberately speculative technology, so
it stays politically and technically constrained:

* It requires paired fixed stations, stable power, thermal management,
  calibration mass, cryptographic custody, and treaty certification.
* It cannot transmit bulk data, live video, remote control of vehicles, or
  battlefield telemetry at useful resolution.
* It cannot move cargo, weapons, fuel, spare parts, personnel, or rescue teams.
* It is expensive enough that message priority is a political decision.
* It produces audit trails. Spoofing, censorship, delay, and priority abuse are
  legal scandals, not invisible magic.
* Frontier worlds often have only one primary Spindle office and several
  lower-trust relay desks, making local capture or outage politically explosive.

Travel constraints:

* No ship can ignore delta-v, heat, reaction mass, maintenance, or braking
  infrastructure.
* Beam corridors are scheduled public utilities, strategic assets, and treaty
  chokepoints.
* Military movements are hard to hide because launch mass, pusher burns, and
  braking reservations leave records.
* Emergency response is physically slow. Reinforcements, inspectors,
  replacement parts, and heavy legal enforcement arrive on transit schedules,
  even when orders and sanctions arrive by Spindle packet first.
* Asterite improves power handling, sensors, capacitors, and beam-array
  durability, but it does not create a shortcut for ships between stars.

This travel model supports the campaign politics. Orison can dominate by owning
freight capacity, insurance, and concession logistics. Sable can project force
only where it has treaty basing and braking rights. Treaty Oversight has real
power because transit permits, Spindle authentication, and beam-slot sanctions
can make or break a frontier economy. Meridian distrusts outside rule because
the people who send orders instantly are often months away from living with the
consequences. It also creates mandate lag: a convoy can depart under one
political settlement and arrive years later to find a different legal and moral
reality.

## Asterite

Asterite is a rare mineral composite found in and around Aster Basin. It is not
fuel and it does not produce energy by itself. It is valuable because refined
Asterite ceramics and dopants improve high-power systems that already need
coolant, feedstock, precision fabrication, skilled maintenance, and stable grid
power.

Primary uses:

* Ultra-low-loss power buses and compact grid storage.
* Rail capacitors, pulsed-power systems, and ruggedized field fabrication
  components.
* Radiation-hardened sensors and electronics for frontier industry.
* Civilian heat taps, pump controllers, and clinic-grade backup power in
  isolated settlements.

Limits:

* Raw Asterite is useful ore, not a finished miracle material.
* Refined Asterite is brittle, heat-sensitive, and export-controlled.
* Asterite-heavy systems can quench, overheat, contaminate coolant, or draw
  emergency power away from civilian grids.
* Military units still need fuel, ammunition, parts, operators, depots, and
  repair windows.
* Field fabrication still needs templates, feedstock, power, trained staff, and
  vulnerable production sites.

## Basin Stabilization Grid

The Basin Stabilization Grid was built by an early public-private consortium to
make Aster Basin livable and profitable. It coordinated pumps, heat taps,
refineries, autonomous depots, emergency shelters, road locks, hazard gates,
and industrial safety systems across the region.

The grid earned the nickname Loom because field technicians saw its routing maps
as interlaced threads: power, water, ore, traffic, settlement heat, maintenance
access, and emergency isolation lines crossing under every valley and ridge.

The grid is dangerous for grounded reasons:

* It controls old but still connected life-support and industrial systems.
* It was mothballed after the Lattice Incident, then partially maintained by
  unofficial Meridian crews.
* Its emergency mode can isolate substations, seal gates, redirect power, lock
  roads, activate maintenance drones, and disable unsafe refineries.
* Its authorization records are fragmented among old consortium keys, Treaty
  Oversight seals, Orison concession archives, Sable security classifications,
  and local Meridian bypasses.
* Armed conflict, illegal siphons, and prototype power draws can trip old safety
  thresholds and spread emergency isolation across the basin.

The grid is not a villain. It does not choose sides. It enforces obsolete rules
with incomplete information while human factions fight over authority.

## Historical Timeline

### 2035-2065: Resource Shock Era

Climate repair, grid hardening, battery shortages, and orbital manufacturing
drive demand for superconductors, high-density storage, and durable industrial
materials. States, corporations, and treaty agencies become more willing to blur
public safety, military readiness, and private extraction.

### 2065-2090: Cislunar And Martian Expansion

Off-world supply chains mature. Transport firms, mining consortia, and security
contractors become quasi-sovereign because remote settlements survive or fail on
logistics.

### 2090-2105: Transit Thread Standardization

The first reliable interstellar beam corridors open between nearby settled
systems. The treaties that certify beam arrays, braking fields, Spindle
stations, mass manifests, data custody, and rescue obligations become the
backbone of interstellar law. Transit operators gain enormous influence because
they control who can move people, parts, auditors, troops, and evidence on
useful timelines, while Spindle operators control whose orders and records get
priority before the ships arrive.

### 2105-2115: Aster Settlement Charter

Caldera's Aster Basin is opened as a charter settlement around rich Asterite
deposits. The first wave builds pump stations, heat taps, autonomous depots,
field refineries, and the Basin Stabilization Grid to support extraction with a
small labor force.

### 2116: The Lattice Incident

A rushed extraction surge overloads part of the grid. A power-routing failure,
mine collapse, refinery lockdown, and security response kill settlers and
contractors. The consortium, treaty agencies, insurers, and participating states
settle quietly. Deep extraction is suspended. Records are sealed. Maps are
edited to turn liability into absence.

### 2117-2135: Quiet Dependency

Free Meridian communities survive by maintaining shallow Asterite taps,
scavenged depots, and local grid bypasses. Outside authorities call the work
illegal. Meridian calls it heat, water, clinics, and winter.

### 2136-2142: Strategic Revaluation

New beam-array components, compact grid storage, and military pulsed-power
systems make refined Asterite strategically decisive again. Orison reopens
concession claims and quietly buys freight slots into Caldera. Sable analysts
classify the basin as a strategic parity concern because Asterite supply can
affect transit infrastructure as much as battlefield hardware. Treaty Oversight
revisits old files and sends carefully worded auditors. Kestrel is deployed as
a civilian survey because a science mission is less provocative than a military
occupation and can fit inside an already scheduled transfer window.

### Campaign Present: The Basin War

Orison moves first with armed claim teams. Sable follows under treaty security
authority. Meridian resists both because outside control threatens civilian
infrastructure. Kestrel starts by defending itself and gradually becomes the
only force trying to keep evidence, settlements, and field truth intact at the
same time.

No outside cavalry can arrive quickly. The inner systems can argue, sanction,
order, threaten, and authenticate evidence almost immediately through the
Spindle Net, but the next heavy convoy is already scheduled, politically
contested, and too far away to solve the opening crisis. That split between
instant authority and slow material help is why local command decisions matter.

Mid-campaign, Caldera receives exactly the type of delayed intervention this
system produces: the Harrowgate Convoy Mandate arrives on schedule carrying
decade-old briefs, modern at-launch hardware, finite reserves, and assumptions
that no longer match basin reality. Late campaign, Helion Interdiction
Directorate packets announce a strategic-denial expedition that treats Asterite
as unacceptable systemic risk and plans overwhelming force to shut the basin
down by destruction if necessary.

## Political Factions

### Kestrel Survey Expedition

Kestrel is officially a civilian science and safety expedition. Its staff
includes geologists, engineers, surveyors, medics, a small security detachment,
and logistics operators. Its political problem is that it was underbriefed by
its sponsors and then forced to make public-safety decisions faster than any
authority can admit what is happening.

Tactical identity: flexible field modifications, repairs, sensors, rescue
pressure, and improvised production.

### Orison Resource Combine

Orison is a vertically integrated extraction, transport, and security company.
Its public argument is plausible: only a scaled industrial operator can reopen
deep Asterite extraction safely, pay for infrastructure restoration, and keep
Caldera economically relevant. Its failure is that it treats concession law,
private security, and public safety as tools for securing market control.

Orison's quiet power is logistical and informational. It owns or leases freight
hulls, beam-slot options, insurance contracts, cargo authentication services,
Spindle priority agreements, and refinery modules already in transit. That lets
Sloane claim that only Orison can prevent economic collapse after Orison creates
the dependency.

Tactical identity: strong economy, fast production, armor pushes, contractors,
mobile refining, and legalistic objective pressure.

### Sable Accord

The Sable Accord is a state-backed security bloc. It fears Orison control of
Asterite will become a military and diplomatic imbalance. Colonel Amara Rhee
represents lawful restraint and strategic caution. Major Lev Kravic represents
the hardline view that critical infrastructure must be seized before rivals can
weaponize it.

Sable power is conditional because expeditionary force requires declared
tonnage, braking reservations, and treaty basing. Rhee cannot simply summon a
fleet into Caldera; she must operate with what arrived on prior schedules and
what the treaty will let her justify.

Tactical identity: disciplined captures, artillery, sensors, layered defense,
and command-zone control.

### Free Meridian Compact

Free Meridian is a network of settlements, haulers, technicians, medics, and
former contract labor communities. It is not a generic rebel faction. Its people
maintain the unofficial infrastructure that keeps homes warm, pumps running,
and clinics powered. They distrust every outside faction because every outside
faction can call their survival illegal.

Tactical identity: fast scouts, ambushes, convoy play, sabotage, local routes,
and terrain knowledge.

### Treaty Oversight Bureau

Treaty Oversight exists to prevent resource wars and industrial disasters. It
has real authority, useful inspectors, and a long institutional memory. It also
has records it would rather keep sealed because reopening the Lattice Incident
would expose decades of legal compromises, insurance settlements, and political
misdirection.

Its hardest tools are not speeches. It can suspend beam-slot certification,
freeze manifests, invalidate evidence custody, force evacuation priority,
revoke Spindle priority, and turn a remote conflict into an interstellar
sanctions case before any new ship arrives.

Campaign role: mission constraints, ceasefire monitors, audit pressure,
evidence custody, evacuation mandates, sanctions, and comic bureaucracy with
real consequences.

### Harrowgate Convoy Mandate

The Harrowgate Convoy Mandate is a scheduled expeditionary force launched under
an older legal framework to "stabilize" Caldera's extraction corridor. Its
command packet left the inner systems nearly a decade before arrival, so its
leaders enter theater with outdated assumptions about Orison legitimacy,
Meridian status, and Kestrel authority.

Its strengths are quality and discipline: modern-at-launch sensors, hardened
logistics doctrine, and elite small-unit training. Its weakness is mass and
replacement depth. It cannot sustain open-ended occupation without local allies,
which makes influence bargaining and political alignment central to campaign
midgame.

Tactical identity: numerically thin elite formations, high information quality,
strict supply discipline, and brittle reserve endurance.

### Helion Interdiction Directorate

Helion is a wealthy inner-system polity that decides Asterite concentration and
grid instability represent an interstellar systemic hazard. Its interdiction
directorate frames destruction as responsible risk management, not conquest.
From Helion's view, denying every actor access to basin-scale Asterite is safer
than negotiating local legitimacy under uncertainty.

Helion does not appear immediately. It first exerts power through Spindle
warnings, legal freezes, insurance withdrawals, freight intimidation, and
evacuation mandates. Its physical force arrives late but overwhelming, creating
a final-arc pressure that can force earlier enemies into temporary coalition.

Tactical identity: heavy interdiction tonnage, long-range denial, logistics
suppression, and deadline-driven escalation.

## Worlds And Locations

### Caldera

Caldera is a harsh frontier world with survivable zones concentrated around
engineered basins, protected valleys, and industrial corridors. Its settlements
depend on pumps, heat taps, imported parts, and local power routing. It is not a
lawless empty world. It is governed by overlapping charters, concessions,
treaty mandates, local compacts, and corporate obligations.

Caldera's orbital infrastructure is modest by inner-system standards: one main
braking reservation hub, a cargo elevator under constant repair, several
private transfer yards, one treaty-certified Spindle office, and lower-trust
relay desks that can be delayed, censored, overloaded, or politically
challenged. Whoever controls manifests, braking priority, and message priority
can decide whether a settlement receives pump bearings, a company receives
armored vehicles, or a legal order gets heard before the battle changes the
facts.

### The Inner Systems

The inner systems are not a single capital. They are dense markets, old states,
shipyards, universities, insurers, courts, and military headquarters connected
by mature Transit Thread routes. They can issue laws, loans, sanctions, and
orders faster than they can understand a frontier crisis.

### Transit Nodes

Transit nodes are orbital ports built around beam arrays, braking fields,
propellant depots, customs courts, quarantine locks, Spindle offices, and
data-authentication relays. They are where corporations lobby, states posture,
inspectors board couriers, and frontier worlds learn which promises actually
made the shipment manifest.

### Aster Basin

Aster Basin is the main campaign theater. It contains Kestrel's HQ valley,
Meridian settlements, Orison refinery corridors, Sable ridge lines, sealed
utility galleries, and old grid control districts. Its strategic value comes
from both Asterite and the infrastructure that makes extraction possible.

### Kestrel HQ Valley

The starting zone is a temporary science camp near a restricted seam, relay mast,
service roads, and the Scout-7 route. It becomes a symbol of the expedition's
forced transition from research to field command.

### Meridian Settlement Grid

The Meridian grid is a lived-in civilian network of heat taps, clinics, garages,
water processors, hauler depots, shelters, and unofficial bypasses. It gives the
campaign human stakes beyond extraction rights.

### Orison Refinery Belt

The refinery belt includes mobile refineries, assay labs, claim offices,
contractor barracks, conveyor roads, coolant loops, and power siphons. It is the
most visible expression of corporate control.

### Sable Fog Line

The fog line is a ridge and antenna frontier used for map control, observation,
sensor warfare, and treaty jurisdiction claims. Sable treats it as proof of
legal authority. Kestrel treats it as evidence that official maps are curated.

### Old Grid District

The old grid district contains sealed pump galleries, buried substations,
autonomous maintenance depots, hazard gates, archived control rooms, and
fragmented authority terminals. It is the physical truth underneath the coverup.

## Technology Rules

* Technology is logistics-bound. Advanced units still need supply, repair,
  transport, cooling, maintenance, and trained operators.
* Interstellar travel uses scheduled beam corridors, fusion pushers, magsail
  braking, depots, and courier relays. It is infrastructure-heavy travel, not
  instant FTL.
* FTL messaging exists through fixed Spindle Net stations, but it is low
  bandwidth, expensive, audited, and politically gated. It cannot move mass,
  cargo, soldiers, fuel, live tactical feeds, or remote rescue.
* Field fabricators are vulnerable industrial tools, not magic printers.
* Autonomous units are maintenance, mining, cargo, or security machines
  repurposed by old protocols or human factions.
* Sensors and fog tools represent line-of-sight, jamming, terrain masking,
  data custody, and comms control rather than supernatural detection.
* CO powers should be framed as doctrine, reserves, command-network timing,
  logistics bursts, emergency repairs, sensor coordination, or morale under
  pressure.
* Any technology that changes a tactical rule must have a visible map object,
  inspect explanation, deterministic behavior, and replay coverage before it
  enters campaign play.

## Campaign Implications

The campaign should pivot from mystery-machine escalation to political and
industrial escalation:

* Missions 1-10 reveal unregistered infrastructure, edited maps, hidden access
  rights, old safety records, and Orison's attempt to reopen extraction before
  public scrutiny can catch up.
* Missions 11-20 focus on who controls roads, depots, pump stations, maps,
  sensors, and legal jurisdiction.
* Missions 21-30 make Meridian civilian dependency central while Orison and
  Sable race to turn Asterite infrastructure into strategic advantage, then the
  Harrowgate convoy arrives with delayed political assumptions and too little
  mass to impose order alone.
* Missions 31-40 become an authority-key war over grid control, emergency
  protocol propagation, evacuation authority, and who can define public safety,
  with every faction competing to align or neutralize Harrowgate influence.
* Missions 41-50 become a public shutdown and governance operation: preserve
  civilian infrastructure, disable unsafe automated enforcement, expose the old
  coverup, prevent any faction from monopolizing the grid, and survive Helion's
  strategic-denial offensive.

## Narrative Rules

* Every Asterite reveal should name a supply-chain cost, legal stakeholder, and
  tactical objective.
* Every interstellar travel reveal should name the route, schedule, cargo mass,
  permit authority, or relay owner that makes the politics concrete.
* Every FTL message reveal should name the sender, priority class, certifying
  office, custody risk, and who benefits if the packet is delayed or promoted.
* Every delayed-convoy reveal should name the launch year, original mandate,
  present mismatch, and who gains by enforcing obsolete assumptions.
* Every grid or Loom reveal should identify who knew, who profited, who carried
  risk, and who loses heat, water, access, or legal protection if the truth is
  mishandled.
* Every Helion escalation beat should appear first as distant pressure, then as
  constrained local consequence, then as direct contact, so emotional stakes
  rise before the final battles.
* The strongest missions turn politics into battlefield verbs: capture records,
  escort inspectors, defend heat taps, disable illegal siphons, protect
  whistleblowers, seize substations, hold ceasefire lines, or keep refineries
  intact because destroying them would harm civilians.
* The ending should not be "destroy the magic machine." A grounded victory is
  disclosure, civilian continuity, safe decommissioning, shared governance, and
  no single-faction monopoly.
