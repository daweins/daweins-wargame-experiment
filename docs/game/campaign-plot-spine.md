## Campaign Premise

The campaign begins as a local survival story. The Kestrel Survey Expedition
arrives in the remote Aster Basin to study a rare superconductive mineral,
nicknamed Asterite by the field team, and expects a season of core samples,
safety forms, and tedious sponsor calls. The first enemy contact turns that
research camp into a static HQ under attack.

Kestrel is not a standing army. It is a civilian expedition with a small
security detachment, a logistics team that knows every missing bolt by name,
and enough prototype equipment to become dangerous when survival leaves no
other choice. Its commanders grow from people trying to get everyone home into
people who can make operational decisions under pressure.

The wider plot expands outward in rings: protect the HQ, secure the valley,
understand the basin, survive rival claims, expose an old infrastructure
coverup, survive a delayed off-world convoy that arrives with decade-old
politics, build a coalition, then prevent any faction from turning public
life-support systems into strategic leverage while an overwhelming destruction
expedition closes in from the inner systems. The player periodically solves a
visible problem and beats the current adversary before the next ring opens.

The wider universe cannot rescue the basin on demand. Interstellar travel runs
through scheduled beam corridors, braking reservations, cargo manifests, and
courier relays. FTL messaging exists through fixed Spindle Net stations, so
orders, sanctions, market shocks, propaganda, and evidence hashes can arrive
quickly. Reinforcements, auditors, replacement parts, and enforcement still
arrive through contested transit windows. That gap between instant authority
and slow material help makes local infrastructure, command judgment, and
evidence custody matter immediately.

## Narrative Constraints

* Keep early stakes personal: hold the HQ, rescue Scout-7, restore comms,
  protect camp infrastructure, and keep the expedition alive.
* Give the player clean victories at regular intervals, especially missions 6,
  10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, and 50.
* Reveal the wider plot through tactical consequences, not long exposition.
  Briefings, map labels, objective updates, radio banter, and debriefs should
  carry most of the story.
* Put character continuity in commanders, staff, rival COs, rescued personnel,
  bases, and recurring locations. Do not rely on persistent named combat units.
* Add one major tactical concept at a time in the first act. Later missions can
  combine systems after the player has seen them in isolation.
* Let humor come from competent people improvising under absurd institutional
  pressure. Do not use jokes to erase losses or undercut survival stakes.
* Use transit delay as narrative pressure. Orders, motives, and legal claims
  carried by arriving fleets can be years out of date when they reach Caldera.
* Build late-game dread through repeated signs of an inbound force before first
  contact: manifest anomalies, relay blackouts, diverted freight, and widened
  evacuation authority.

## Asterite Industrial Coverup

Asterite first appears to be a rare mineral deposit with unusual industrial
value. It is not fuel and it does not produce energy by itself. Refined
Asterite ceramics and dopants make compact power buses, rail capacitors,
ruggedized sensors, and field fabrication systems more efficient, but every use
still depends on mining, refining, coolant, grid power, feedstock, trained
operators, and vulnerable supply lines.

Orison wants Asterite because it can control the extraction supply chain. Sable
wants it because Orison control would shift military parity and transit
infrastructure bargaining power. Meridian communities already rely on shallow
Asterite taps for heat, pumps, clinics, and repair shops. Kestrel initially
thinks it is studying ore quality, then learns the real dispute is about
infrastructure authority, freight dependency, and decades of hidden liability.

The Loom is a field nickname for the Basin Stabilization Grid: an old
human-built industrial-control mesh that once coordinated pumps, heat taps,
refineries, autonomous depots, road locks, and emergency isolation systems
across Aster Basin. It is not alien, sentient, or limitless. It is damaged
infrastructure with fragmented authorization records and obsolete emergency
logic.

The truth emerges in stages:

* Missions 1-4 show that Asterite has immediate military and civilian value.
* Missions 5-10 reveal that Orison, Sable, Kestrel's sponsors, and Treaty
  Oversight each knew part of the old infrastructure story before Kestrel
  arrived.
* Missions 11-20 show edited maps, unregistered utility corridors, sensor gaps,
  and jurisdiction claims around the old grid.
* Missions 21-30 reveal that modern extraction and prototype testing are
  stressing grid systems that Meridian settlements still depend on, while a
  scheduled convoy arrives with decade-old political assumptions.
* Missions 31-40 turn the conflict into an authority-key war over who can
  control substations, depots, refineries, emergency locks, evacuation
  mandates, and convoy alignment.
* Missions 41-50 force the coalition to place the grid under public control,
  disable unsafe automated enforcement, preserve civilian utilities, expose the
  old coverup, and survive an incoming off-world destruction expedition.

The broader universe history, political order, technology limits, and grounded
rules for Asterite and the Loom are defined in
[Universe Backstory](universe-backstory.md).

## Missions 1-10

### Mission 1: Scout-7 Is Late

Mission-start cinematic and gameplay handoff details are documented in
[Intro Cutscene And Game Start](intro-cutscene-game-start.md).

The campaign opens during a routine camp alert. Scout-7 misses check-in after
surveying a restricted seam, the camp relay is jammed, and unmarked Orison units
move toward the expedition HQ. Venn still thinks of the situation as an armed
evacuation drill until the first enemy unit tries to cross the chokepoint.

* Tactical objective: Hold the HQ, rescue or protect Scout-7, then defeat the
  remaining enemies.
* Starting stakes: Personal survival for the expedition camp.
* Main adversary: Unmarked Orison claim team.
* Tactical lesson: Chokepoint defense, terrain cover, direct attacks,
  counterattacks, and HQ loss conditions.
* Story reveal: Scout-7 saw armed contractors around a seam that was supposed
  to be geologically uninteresting.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel survives the first raid and proves the camp can
  fight long enough to organize.
* Tone beat: Venn calls it "a lab safety drill with live ammunition," then
  asks whether that went out over open comms.

### Mission 2: Inventory Adjustment

Kestrel needs to restore contact with its orbital relay and recover enough fuel
to move the survey crawlers. Orison contractors are already at the relay because
they knew the maintenance schedule, which means the attack came from inside the
project's shared logistics data.

* Tactical objective: Capture or hold the comms relay and fuel cache before
  Orison can take both.
* Starting stakes: The camp can survive one raid, but it cannot survive being
  isolated and out of fuel.
* Main adversary: Orison contractors with light vehicles and infantry.
* Tactical lesson: Capture units, property control, and splitting attention
  between two nearby objectives.
* Story reveal: Orison had access to Kestrel's relay timetable.
* Player victory beat: The relay comes back online, and Kestrel sends a narrow
  emergency burst before the channel is jammed again.
* Tone beat: Priya reclassifies ammunition as "rapidly depreciating research
  consumables" to get it through the inventory system.

### Mission 3: Road To Pump Station Three

The camp water and coolant loop depends on Pump Station Three, a civilian
facility linked by exposed service roads. Kestrel must move damaged survey
crawlers and engineers through ambush country while keeping Orison from
occupying the station.

* Tactical objective: Escort damaged crawlers to the pump station, block ambush
  routes, and hold the station through the enemy response.
* Starting stakes: The expedition will lose heat, water, and repair capacity if
  the pump station falls.
* Main adversary: Orison light vehicles using roads for fast flanking.
* Tactical lesson: Road speed, exposed movement, blocking, and convoy pressure.
* Story reveal: Pump filters contain Asterite residue because the station is
  tied into an old utility corridor missing from official maps.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel saves the camp infrastructure and gains a first
  clue that the deposits do not behave like normal ore.
* Tone beat: Convoy vehicles are named after rejected grant proposals, because
  the engineering team "already mourned them once."

### Mission 4: Emergency Fabrication Authority

Rusk argues that Kestrel cannot keep fighting with recovered survey vehicles and
whatever Priya can weld before breakfast. The team activates a field
fabricator, but Orison moves to seize the depots that feed it. Venn signs an
emergency authority order that makes the expedition a militia in everything but
the word itself.

* Tactical objective: Capture two depots, activate the field fabricator, then
  defeat the enemy HQ guard.
* Starting stakes: Kestrel must turn survival into repeatable defense.
* Main adversary: Orison armored detachment.
* Tactical lesson: First controlled base-production mission, income pressure,
  and reinforced enemy positions.
* Story reveal: Orison has filed a legal claim over the entire Aster Basin.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel gains limited production and stops waiting to be
  rescued.
* Tone beat: Rusk asks for rules of engagement. Venn answers, "Apparently we
  are editing them live."

### Mission 5: Fog Over Antenna Field

The emergency burst from Mission 2 draws help, but not the kind Kestrel expects.
Sable Accord recon units enter the basin under treaty authority and begin
mapping the same deposits. A Kestrel science team is trapped near antenna
stations during a sensor blackout, and Holt pushes for a rescue instead of a
withdrawal.

* Tactical objective: Use soft fog and repaired antenna stations to locate the
  trapped team, extract them, and prevent Sable from taking the field.
* Starting stakes: Kestrel is no longer fighting one corporation. A state-backed
  military has arrived.
* Main adversary: Sable Accord recon force.
* Tactical lesson: Soft fog, sensor coverage, and recon units.
* Story reveal: Sable has partial Asterite maps that predate Kestrel's survey.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel saves the team and learns enough to challenge
  Sable's claim.
* Tone beat: Holt insists the fog is "professionally inconvenient," not
  mystical, which becomes less comforting every turn.

### Mission 6: Bridge Warranty Void

Orison tries to cut the valley in half by demolishing the only bridge that can
carry heavy vehicles. Kestrel must seize the bridge, stop the demolition crews,
and counterattack across it before Orison can regroup. This mission gives the
first clean tactical win over the opening adversary.

* Tactical objective: Seize the bridge, prevent demolition, then break the
  Orison force on the far bank.
* Starting stakes: The home valley either becomes a defensible perimeter or a
  set of isolated camps.
* Main adversary: Orison regional commander with demolition infantry and armor.
* Tactical lesson: Timed pressure without a hard turn limit, bridge chokepoints,
  and counteroffensive positioning.
* Story reveal: Orison's local command depends on one road and one extraction
  schedule.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel secures the home valley and beats the first
  Orison push.
* Tone beat: Priya notes that the bridge warranty excludes "hostile peer
  review."

### Mission 7: Peer Review With Rockets

Kestrel pushes beyond the secured valley and meets the Free Meridian Compact,
locals who have lived around the basin for years. Calder attacks a Kestrel
drill because she thinks every outside faction is here to strip the valley and
leave civilians with the bill.

* Tactical objective: Protect the survey drill and Asterite crates while
  disabling enough Meridian units to force talks.
* Starting stakes: The player learns that not every opponent is a villain and
  not every objective should be solved by capture alone.
* Main adversary: Free Meridian Compact raiders and scouts.
* Tactical lesson: Fast enemies, terrain tricks, and restraint as a mission
  framing device.
* Story reveal: Meridian settlements use small Asterite taps for power, heat,
  and medical systems.
* Player victory beat: Calder agrees to talk because Kestrel protects the drill
  site without destroying the settlement grid.
* Tone beat: Calder calls Kestrel "tourists with artillery and better
  stationery."

### Mission 8: The Audit Arrives

A basin-wide blackout hits as Treaty Oversight Bureau personnel arrive to audit
the emergency militarization order. Orison remnants use the blackout to strike
the supply hub, while Sable units probe nearby. The audit team is useless in a
fight but very good at revealing that Kestrel's sponsor knew Asterite had
military value.

* Tactical objective: Defend the HQ and supply hub, escort engineers to restore
  power, and keep the audit convoy alive.
* Starting stakes: Kestrel's internal trust cracks. The expedition may have
  been underbriefed by its own sponsors.
* Main adversary: Orison remnants, with Sable pressure on the edge of the map.
* Tactical lesson: Multi-objective defense, repair escorts, and supply hub
  protection.
* Story reveal: The sponsor classified Asterite as strategically sensitive
  before Kestrel deployed.
* Player victory beat: Power returns, the hub survives, and Venn forces the
  auditor to unlock sealed project files.
* Tone beat: The auditor asks whether the battle has a sign-in sheet.

### Mission 9: Blank Map Territory

The sealed files point to a ridge line that appears blank on official maps.
Kestrel enters through fog and jamming, capturing sensor posts while Sable tries
to stop the expedition from proving the maps were altered. The mission turns
the campaign from a resource fight into a mystery.

* Tactical objective: Break through the fogged ridge, capture sensor posts,
  destroy jammers, and escape with scan data.
* Starting stakes: The basin may have been deliberately misrepresented for
  years.
* Main adversary: Sable Accord sensor and artillery force.
* Tactical lesson: Fog, jammers, sensor captures, and artillery avoidance.
* Story reveal: Official maps omit old power, pump, and ore-routing corridors
  that cut across the basin beneath modern concession boundaries.
* Player victory beat: Kestrel proves the blank map is a lie and gets the first
  complete grid-route fragment.
* Tone beat: Venn describes the old map as "optimistic fiction with contour
  lines."

### Mission 10: Operation Small Print

Kestrel hits Orison's refinery before Sloane can extract the richest Asterite
cache and leave everyone else fighting over damage. This is the first full
production battle and the first major antagonist defeat. The refinery falls,
but its emergency broadcast sends the grid-routing map to every faction in the
region.

* Tactical objective: Capture Orison's refinery HQ or rout Sloane's command
  force while protecting Kestrel's own HQ.
* Starting stakes: Defeat Orison's opening campaign before the basin becomes a
  permanent extraction war.
* Main adversary: Director Cassian Sloane and Orison refinery security.
* Tactical lesson: Full production, HQ pressure, income control, and decisive
  enemy commander defeat.
* Story reveal: The refinery was illegally drawing power through old grid
  routing, not only mining ore.
* Player victory beat: Orison loses the local refinery and Sloane retreats with
  his legal strategy on fire.
* Tone beat: The victory debrief is interrupted by an abandoned Orison server
  auto-playing a compliance webinar.

## Five-Mission Arcs To Mission 50

### Missions 11-15: Basin Stabilization

Kestrel turns the home valley victory into a real operating perimeter. The
stakes rise from camp survival to regional control, but the missions give the
player a short consolidation burst after beating Orison's first campaign.

* Mission 11, Second Camp Syndrome: Capture an abandoned survey camp and turn it
  into a forward base while Orison contractors try to burn the stores.
* Mission 12, Depot Math: Secure three depots that create income and supply
  choices, introducing sharper production tradeoffs.
* Mission 13, Hilltop Receiver: Take a ridge receiver under indirect fire and
  unlock the first artillery-style support unit.
* Mission 14, Evacuation Lane: Protect Meridian civilian transports through a
  road network while choosing which properties to hold.
* Mission 15, Perimeter Day: Defend three approaches at once and push Orison
  remnants out of the basin perimeter.

Clean victory: Kestrel owns a defensible operating zone and can choose where to
strike next.

### Missions 16-20: The Fog Line

Sable becomes the main rival across ridges, antenna fields, and sensor-blind
valleys. The stakes rise from territory to truth: whoever controls the maps can
define the war.

* Mission 16, Courtesy Of The Accord: Rhee opens with a formal seizure order,
  teaching disciplined enemy captures and recon screens.
* Mission 17, Jammer Etiquette: Destroy jammers before they hide a Sable
  artillery line.
* Mission 18, Silent Grid: Investigate a sensor anomaly where Asterite seams
  interfere with both sides and introduce limited neutral hazards.
* Mission 19, Ridge Doctrine: Break a layered Sable defense that teaches
  artillery counterplay and terrain patience.
* Mission 20, Ceasefire Weather: Capture corrupted map evidence before Kravic
  can destroy it, forcing Rhee into a limited ceasefire.

Clean victory: Kestrel beats the Sable information advantage and earns a
professional rival's respect.

### Missions 21-25: The Meridian Question

The campaign shifts from rival armies to civilian consequence. Meridian is not
strong enough to win the basin war alone, but its people understand the terrain
and what Asterite extraction costs.

* Mission 21, No One Lives On Empty Land: Protect a settlement caught between
  Kestrel and Sable fire lanes.
* Mission 22, Convoy Without Flags: Escort a Meridian medical convoy while
  Orison remnants try to seize its Asterite batteries.
* Mission 23, Ambush Permits: Survive Calder's terrain traps and prove Kestrel
  can fight without flattening her settlement grid.
* Mission 24, Blackout Treaty: Restore power to civilian heat taps while
  rationing production and repairs.
* Mission 25, Common Cause: Defend the largest Meridian camp from a combined
  Orison and Sable splinter attack.

Clean victory: Calder joins a loose coalition, and Kestrel gains faster scout
and skirmisher options.

### Missions 26-30: Prototype Race

Every faction starts fielding Asterite-enhanced units before the safety and
legal limits of the old grid are public. The stakes rise from regional control
to technological and political escalation. Mid-arc, a scheduled Harrowgate
convoy arrives with decade-old assumptions: Orison still legitimate, Meridian
classified as irregulars, and Kestrel treated as a temporary contractor.

* Mission 26, First Asterite Frame: Face the first prototype armor and learn
  that standard counters still matter.
* Mission 27, Countermeasure Field Trip: Protect Kestrel engineers while they
  test anti-prototype tools under fire and identify unknown long-range beacon
  pings on a legal transit schedule.
* Mission 28, The Fleet In The Filing Cabinet: Meet the Harrowgate convoy when
  it arrives on time with ten-year-old treaty briefs and immediate demands for
  custody of basin infrastructure.
* Mission 29, Four Parties, One Road: Race Orison, Sable, Meridian splinters,
  and Harrowgate advance units to a cache without letting any one faction
  snowball.
* Mission 30, The Engine Below: Defeat Sloane's mobile refinery and discover it
  was hijacking old grid power-routing and tripping emergency lockouts.

Clean victory: Sloane is beaten as a front-line antagonist. Wider consequence:
the hidden infrastructure problem becomes public enough that every faction
tries to control the explanation.

### Missions 31-35: Emergency Protocol War

Emergency grid protocols begin isolating substations, sealing routes, activating
old maintenance drones, and locking depots after repeated unauthorized draws and
combat damage. Former enemies and new convoy arrivals blame each other before
the battlefield proves the threat is systemic infrastructure failure under fire.

* Mission 31, Unclaimed Contact: Fight the first grid maintenance drones while
  all human factions misidentify them as enemy prototypes.
* Mission 32, Factory That Wasn't There: Shut down an automated depot before it
  locks out every nearby base.
* Mission 33, Rhee's Line: Hold a settlement with Sable and Kestrel units on the
  same side of the map while Harrowgate convoy officers contest command
  authority in real time.
* Mission 34, Calder's Evacuation: Escort civilian convoys through drone
  pressure and collapsing routes while deciding which Harrowgate shipment gets
  docking priority.
* Mission 35, Basin Shield: Defend the largest settlement long enough for Priya
  to build a regional shield relay and force a first operational ceasefire among
  Kestrel, Sable, Meridian, and Harrowgate.

Clean victory: The coalition saves the settlement and proves human factions can
coordinate without hidden cheating or narrative handwaving, even when their
legal mandates were written in different decades.

### Missions 36-40: The Hardliner Offensive

Kravic and Orison remnants decide the Basin Stabilization Grid should be seized
rather than placed under public oversight. The stakes rise from survival to
control of infrastructure authority that can dictate power, roads, depots,
evacuations, and resource claims.

* Mission 36, Control Is A Verb: Stop Kravic from taking the first authority
  uplink.
* Mission 37, Terms And Batteries: Break an Orison remnant economy that uses
  Asterite stockpiles to flood the map with cheap armor.
* Mission 38, Relay Cascade: Capture uplinks in sequence before the hardliners
  chain them together.
* Mission 39, Doctrine In The Dark: Rusk leads a fog-heavy defense that proves
  disciplined planning and improvisation can coexist.
* Mission 40, The Containment Lie: Defeat Kravic at the uplink and learn that
  the grid's emergency powers were expanded after the Lattice Incident, then
  sealed to avoid liability.

Clean victory: Kravic loses his bid to control the network. Wider consequence:
the coalition inherits responsibility for public disclosure and safe operation
of infrastructure that settlements still need.

### Missions 41-45: Grid Collapse

The grid crisis shifts from localized lockouts to basin-wide instability.
Power islands, factory lockouts, pump failures, drone security zones, and
settlement brownouts spread as factions keep forcing the system beyond its
damaged authority model. At the same time, Helion Interdiction packets begin
arriving through the Spindle Net: formal warnings that Caldera may be placed
under mandatory strategic denial.

* Mission 41, Correction Cycle: Survive a security-zone cascade that changes
  pressure routes through automated depots while Helion issues a first remote
  freeze order on Asterite shipments.
* Mission 42, No Such Thing As Neutral Ground: Capture factories before grid
  lockout makes them unusable and hold evidence depots before they can be
  legally reclassified as interdiction targets.
* Mission 43, Nayar's Worst Diagram: Chain power relays across the map while
  defending engineers under worsening orbital traffic shutdowns.
* Mission 44, The Map Draws Back: Push through barriers and chokepoints that
  make the battlefield feel like the grid is failing in patterns while long
  range contacts begin staging outside Caldera's braking envelope.
* Mission 45, Regional Grid: Stabilize the regional grid branch, saving the
  basin but leaving central authority unresolved as Helion's main force commits
  to approach.

Clean victory: The region survives, and the coalition gets a path to the old
grid control district before the off-world deadline closes.

### Missions 46-50: Containment Protocol

The final arc brings every unlocked system together: production, HQ stakes,
logistics, fog, advanced units, convoy protection, captures, and authority-node
shutdowns. Helion's overwhelming interdiction force enters theater and turns the
old basin war into a shared survival problem. Mission 50 mirrors Mission 1 at a
larger scale.

* Mission 46, Borrowed Armies: Lead a coalition force where each allied faction,
  including Harrowgate convoy veterans, contributes a tactical strength without
  adding multiplayer control.
* Mission 47, The Last Supply Run: Protect the final convoy carrying coolant,
  batteries, and field parts to the control-district approach while Helion
  interdiction wings strike logistics corridors.
* Mission 48, The First Survey Site: Return to the starting valley and defend
  the original camp against a full grid security cascade and first direct Helion
  ground assault.
* Mission 49, Command HQ: Hold the coalition HQ and protect the science team
  while they prepare the shutdown command under timed orbital-denial pressure.
* Mission 50, Scout-7 Is On Time: Push through the control-district defenses,
  protect Holt's recon team, repel Helion's final destruction push, and place
  the grid under transparent coalition authority without letting any faction
  capture it.

Clean victory: Former enemies preserve civilian infrastructure together,
disable unsafe automated enforcement, expose the coverup, and prevent both a
single-faction grid monopoly and off-world strategic destruction. Ending text
can vary by score, optional objectives, and saved infrastructure without
requiring branching cutscenes or persistent named combat units.

## Implementation Notes

The first 10 missions are the campaign act most suitable for early prototyping.
Each should eventually become a mission brief with map ingredients, unit lists,
enemy behavior, objective text, six to eight radio lines, and debrief copy.
Environmental identity, terrain kit planning, mission looks, and stage-level
visual progression are defined in
[Campaign Environment Plan](campaign-environment-plan.md).
Character backgrounds, relationships, commander doctrine, voice examples,
candidate CO powers, and signature-unit guardrails are defined in
[Campaign Character Bible](campaign-character-bible.md).

Missions 11-50 are a modular spine, not a locked content contract. Each
five-mission arc should survive cutting or replacement of one mission without
collapsing the whole campaign. If a mission requires a new faction, new unit
family, new terrain set, new objective script, new AI behavior, and a bespoke
cutscene, split it into smaller work.

The next narrative implementation slice should turn Missions 1-3 into playable
mission briefs that fit the existing prototype systems before expanding to new
rules such as production, fog, or supply.
